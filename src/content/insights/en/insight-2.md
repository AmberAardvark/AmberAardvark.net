Data, automation, and application projects cross team boundaries. The people who understand the process, the data, the systems, and the risk often have different perspectives, so effective delivery depends on bringing those perspectives together early.

## Build a Shared Problem Statement

Begin with the desired outcome and the current process rather than a preferred tool. A shared problem statement should capture the bottleneck, affected users, available systems, constraints, and evidence that will be used to evaluate a solution.

## Make Decisions Visible

Project decisions should be recorded with enough context for another person to understand them later. This includes assumptions, tradeoffs, owners, dependencies, and the conditions that would cause a decision to be revisited.

## Include Operations Early

Testing, support, permissions, monitoring, and recovery are part of the product. Involving the people responsible for those concerns during design helps expose practical gaps before they become production problems.

## Create Feedback Loops

Short delivery cycles, demonstrations, and reviewable prototypes make it easier to test whether a workflow matches reality. Feedback should be tied to concrete behavior, such as a processed record, a failed handoff, or an approval path.

## Collaboration as a Delivery Practice

Collaboration is most useful when it changes decisions and reduces uncertainty. Clear responsibilities, accessible documentation, and regular review points give a project a more dependable way to move from requirements to a maintainable solution.
